1 · The deployment study (the DiD) — Hoodi vs a never-treated control

The study everyone would want — a staggered difference-in-differences of mump2p adoption on mainnet — is impossible: all seven partner operators were named in a single press release (2025-06-24), the product never left testnet, and two of the seven never publicly claimed adoption at all. There is no staggering and no treatment in mainnet data.

But mump2p did deploy on the Hoodi testnet ("rolling out this summer"; first vendor results 2025-09-23). So we ran the honest version: 487 network-days of daily propagation physics, Hoodi against Sepolia — same clients, same forks, no mump2p — scanning for a structural break instead of assuming a date.

Findings.

  • No positive mump2p signature exists. The best break in Hoodi's transit (−56%, 2025-09-29) is matched three days later on the untreated control — harder (−72%) and cleaner (r² 0.84 vs 0.34). It is a common shock (the Fusaka client-release window plus sentry churn), not a treatment effect. Every clean break in eight months belongs to the network without mump2p.
  • During the deployment window the treated network got worse. Hoodi's transit tripled Aug 1 – Oct 1 in a clean square pulse while the control stayed flat — with stable sentry composition, so it is not an observation artifact. We cannot attribute the degradation to any specific experiment; we note only that the vendor's "6× vs gossipsub" benchmark was captured inside this window, against Hoodi's worst gossipsub conditions of the entire study period.
  • The benchmark venue flatters the claim. Hoodi's baseline gossipsub transit (~1,900 ms in mid-2025) is more than twice mainnet's (~880 ms). A 6× win over a slow testnet baseline implies far less against the fast network the product is actually sold for.
  • On the mump2p network today (June 2026): the attestation cliff is fully intact (95.9% → 77.4% → 40.5% across the deadline), 2.1% of blocks arrive late — more than double mainnet's 0.93% — and the attestation floor is ~20× worse than mainnet.

This is no evidence of effect, not proof of none — their own methodology says blocks propagated "through both mump2p and Gossipsub simultaneously" (shadow mode), which predicts exactly this null. But for a product whose pitch is propagation speed, leaving no visible trace on its only deployed network is the most load-bearing fact on this page.

2 · The mainnet physics: latency is free until it isn't

Attesters must vote 4,000 ms into the slot for whatever head they can see. RANDAO assigns them to slots at random, so exposure to a late block is as-good-as-random — comparing the same validators across early-vs-late blocks is causal, not correlational. From 216,000 mainnet slots:

4–5s — past deadline1,84867.43%
5–6s15722.35%
> 6s650.12%

Three facts that reframe the product:

  • 61% of head-vote misses happen on blocks that arrived on time — they are nodes in the propagation tail. The attester benefit of a faster transport is being pulled out of the tail on every block, not rescuing rare late ones.
  • Late blocks are published late, not transported slowly. Late blocks' median publication is 3,656 ms (vs 1,811 ms normal); 18% were published after the deadline already. Even an infinitely fast network rescues only ~80% of them; 2× rescues 1.5%.
  • Orphans are timing-game losers. 188 orphaned blocks in 30 days; hazard is 0.04% before the deadline, 9.9% at 4.5–5s, 28.5% at 5–6s. Their transit was barely worse than survivors' (482 vs 322 ms) — their publication was 2.4s later.

4 · The adoption curve has a sweet spot

Channel C needs receivers (a proposer can only delay safely once ~40% of attesters get blocks fast — the proposer-boost threshold) and decays as adopting predecessors steal the same flow. Per-adopter value = C × min(1, α/40%) × (1−α):

  • The seven named partners (16.8% of the network) sit below the sweet spot — recruiting more of their competitors would make each of them richer, up to ~40%.
  • The adopt-vs-not spread saturates at ~$32/validator and never decays: late adoption is bought to stop bleeding, not to gain. At 100%, the MEV channel is fully cannibalised and only the public goods remain (~$3.30/validator).
  • Universal adoption doesn't bank the saved milliseconds — it spends them: every proposer moves to the wire, and the network gets faster and more fragile simultaneously. The durable social value is protocol headroom (more blobs, shorter slots), which accrues to L2s and users, not operators.

5 · Extrapolating Hoodi onto mainnet: the floor model

The naive transfer — "6× on Hoodi ⇒ 6× on mainnet" — fails on our own measurement: Hoodi's gossipsub baseline is ~2× slower than mainnet's. What an RLNC overlay physically delivers is not a multiplier but an absolute latency floor (~150 ms at Hoodi gateways, per the vendor), and since it runs parallel to gossipsub a node takes whichever arrives first: transit = min(observed, floor). The same floor buys very different multipliers on different networks:

Mainnet's median node is already near the floor. At 331 ms typical transit, gossipsub on mainnet is nearly as fast as the overlay claims to be — the headline 6× exists only against slow baselines. What survives the transfer is tail compression: the p90 spread (976 ms → floor) that a proposer must cover before spending delay budget. Priced against the 214k-slot mainnet panel:

Stated limitations. Arrival is a sentry-median, so per-attester measurement error smooths the deadline discontinuity — sharp-RD estimates are bandwidth-sensitive and we lead with the dose-response instead. The 6× speedup is the vendor's testnet claim, not a mainnet measurement (hence 3× and 2× scenarios). Fleet node counts and the 40% safety threshold are stated assumptions.
